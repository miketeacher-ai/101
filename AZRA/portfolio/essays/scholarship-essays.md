## Essay 1: Leadership and Community Impact

The permission meeting for our school courtyard build lasted eleven minutes and contained one question I still think about. The vice principal asked why students would volunteer to saw wood in August heat when they could be on phones. I showed her my sketchbook: twenty pages of classmates' own ideas for a reading nook, collected during my workshop. "Because it's theirs," I said. She signed.

That answer summarizes Tasarım ve Paylaş, the workshop I founded this summer. Five sessions, twenty-plus participants, zero budget: we studied Balat's facades with borrowed measuring tapes, modeled bus stops from cardboard, argued about what makes public space feel safe for women after dark — a question Turkish cities are finally asking out loud. Every session ended with participants redesigning one real spot in our neighborhood. Best proposals fed into our build.

The nook taught harder lessons. First frame failed the wobble test; reclaimed pallets hid nails that ate two blades; paint dried blotchy under Istanbul humidity. Eight of us rebuilt twice. When younger students used the finished nook before we cleaned up, I understood leadership is creating conditions where other people's ownership becomes inevitable.

I apply with full need because impact should not depend on family income. Fund my training, and pattern multiplies: more workshops, more builds, more communities discovering experts they waited for were themselves.

## Essay 2: Financial Need and Commitment

My grandmother survived 1999 in a building that didn't protect her neighbors. She talks about the date only through architecture: which walls cracked, which stairwell held, how the doorway she sheltered in became sacred. I inherited her stories and her country's unfinished argument with its own buildings — Turkey has seismic codes and seismic realities, gap measured in lives.

Full financial aid is the only route by which I can study at the level that gap demands. My family is generous with everything except money; resources went into keeping me in good schools and pencils in my hand. American tuition figures read like fiction. So I state need without embarrassment: fund me fully, or not at all — half solutions produce dropouts, and I refuse.

What you receive is someone who has begun. In forty-five days I built a three-project portfolio, founded a twenty-person workshop, led a community build from proposal to install. My sketchbook holds years of measured drawings of Istanbul — the city I intend to serve as architect specializing in resilient public space, retrofitting trust into buildings one neighborhood at a time.

Aid will flow back across the Bosphorus for forty years: safer schools, braver plazas, students taught that design is citizenship.
