# Supplement - Cooper Union (Chanin School of Architecture)

**Student:** Azra | **Major:** Architecture | **Sprint:** Day 1 Mon Aug 24 - Day 45 Wed Oct 7, 2026

## Draft (~250 words)

Cooper Union's architecture program begins with a proposition that has organized my last year: a city can be read, measured, and answered like a text. My text has been Istanbul — specifically, a crack in my grandmother's Balat apartment that has taught me more about load paths than any textbook, and a Kadıköy bus stop where thirty people daily choreograph survival around a design failure.

That attention to the everyday as the site of architecture is why the Chanin School attracts me. First, its foundation year devoted to drawing and making before specialization matches how I already work: my portfolio's strongest pages are not renders but measured sketches at 1:50 with material notes, and my self-taught SketchUp models improved only after my hand understood the joint. Cooper's insistence on analog intelligence before digital fluency is the training my practice begs for.

Second, Cooper's history of full-tuition commitment — even as its current structure evolves (verify) — signals a community that takes access seriously. As a full-aid international applicant who built a courtyard library nook from reclaimed pallets because there was no budget, I recognize that ethic. My Tasarım ve Paylaş workshop for twenty peers operated on the same principle: excellent design should not require excellent funding, only excellent observation.

Third, the home test's invitation to respond without a portfolio-school polish feels honest to a student whose best work was made on school computers after hours. I arrive with three projects — a micro-apartment, a bus stop, a seismic pavilion — and a habit of checking my grandmother's crack with a pencil for scale. I want to leave Cooper able to draw buildings that would have held her neighbors.
