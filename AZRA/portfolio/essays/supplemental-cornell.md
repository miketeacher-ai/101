# Supplement - Cornell AAP (Why Architecture)

**Student:** Azra | **Major:** Architecture | **Sprint:** Day 1 Mon Aug 24 - Day 45 Wed Oct 7, 2026

## Draft (~250 words)

Cornell AAP asks applicants to be precise about why architecture, not just why Cornell. My answer begins with a crack in a Balat wall and a question about trust: what does it take for a building to keep its promise when the ground moves? My family answers that question through stories of 1999; I have tried to answer it through drawings, then models, then a small building of my own.

That trajectory is why AAP fits. First, the B.Arch's integration of design studio with structures, history, and theory mirrors how my projects already operate: my earthquake-resilient pavilion required me to research Turkey's seismic codes and Japanese precedents before I could justify a single massing move. AAP does not let concept float free of consequence — exactly the discipline my self-taught practice needs.

Second, AAP's introduction to analog craft before digital dominance appeals to a student who learned architecture by measuring a kitchen with a borrowed tape. My sketchbook — three years of Istanbul streetscapes drawn at 1:50 with dimensions and material notes — is not nostalgia; it is evidence that I think through my hands. I want four more years of that, alongside peers who also believe a well-drawn section is an ethical act.

Third, AAP's field presence in New York City and Rome offers a way to test my Balat-trained eye against other layered historic cities. I arrive with a portfolio of a micro-apartment retrofit, a bus stop redesign, and a seismic pavilion, plus a community workshop I founded for twenty peers. I want to leave AAP able to design public spaces that deserve the people who wait in them.
