# Supplement - Duke University (Why Duke / Community)

**Student:** Elfin | **Major:** Molecular Biology & Medicine | **Sprint:** Day 1 Mon Aug 24 - Day 45 Wed Oct 7, 2026

## Draft (~250 words)

Duke's Karsh International Scholarship description reads like my last year written as a selection profile: academic excellence, leadership, cross-cultural engagement, commitment to return. I didn't plan that; I lived it — carrying my grandmother to village clinics, teaching myself code to extend one doctor's reach, then organizing a health fair that measured 127 neighbors' blood pressure with nursing-student volunteers. Duke is where I want to learn whether that pattern scales.

Three specifics anchor my interest. First, Duke's emphasis on collaborative research rather than competitive pre-med culture matches how my projects succeeded: my computational biology work improved when I asked a peer to break my figures, and my health fair succeeded because I delegated stations to volunteers who knew more than I did about blood-pressure cuffs. I want four years of that interdependence.

Second, the Duke clinical ecosystem — proximity to Duke University Hospital and extensive undergraduate shadowing and research integration — promises what my twenty-hour poliklinik observation could only gesture at: sustained, mentored clinical learning alongside molecular biology coursework. My paper explores proteins; my shadowing taught me patients; Duke integrates both from year one.

Third, Duke's civic engagement through Durham offers a way to test my Turkey-trained community health model in a new context. I arrive with a curriculum for health education booths that raised quiz scores 25% in my town, ready to adapt. And I arrive knowing how to ask permission from a muhtar — a skill that translates.

Karsh would make this possible. Without it, as a full-need international student, Duke is unreachable. With it, my grandmother's bag of documents becomes a research agenda: making care travel.
