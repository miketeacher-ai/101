# Supplement - Emory University (Why Emory)

**Student:** Elfin | **Major:** Molecular Biology & Medicine | **Sprint:** Day 1 Mon Aug 24 - Day 45 Wed Oct 7, 2026

## Draft (~250 words)

My most important classroom was a poliklinik hallway where I watched one doctor serve five villages and learned that medicine's scarcest resource is not knowledge but time. That hallways's lesson is why Emory attracts me — a university where pre-health education is not a track but an ecosystem.

Three specifics pull me. First, Emory's partnership with the CDC — literally next door — offers a way to study the population-level patterns my health fair only glimpsed at the individual level. My one-day fair measured 127 neighbors' blood pressure and taught me that education booths matter as much as cuffs; the CDC studies why such fairs matter at city scale. I want to learn both.

Second, Emory College's emphasis on undergraduate research with faculty mentorship matches how my sprint succeeded: my computational project exploring protein-ligand docking improved when a teacher challenged my first figure, and my shadowing reflection sharpened when a nurse asked what I had actually observed, not what I assumed. Emory promises four years of that mentored iteration.

Third, the Emory Scholars community's focus on service resonates with a student whose projects have always been public: a GitHub repo anyone can reproduce, a health fair anyone in my town could attend, a pill-reminder program I gave to my grandmother rather than kept as a private project.

I arrive with a paper, a repo, a shadowing log, and a fair's evidence. I want to leave Emory — perhaps via its Oxford campus option — able to extend one doctor's reach to a county, not just a waiting room.
