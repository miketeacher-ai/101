# Supplement - Harvard University (Optional Essay)

**Student:** Elfin | **Major:** Molecular Biology & Medicine | **Sprint:** Day 1 Mon Aug 24 - Day 45 Wed Oct 7, 2026

## Draft (~250 words)

Harvard's optional supplement invites what is not elsewhere in the application — for me, that is permission. Literally. My most consequential projects began with asking a muhtar for a health fair venue, a poliklinik chief for shadowing hours, a grandmother for trust to reorganize her pills. I have learned that initiative without permission is performance; permission without initiative is waiting.

Three specifics pull me to Harvard, beyond the need-blind promise that makes my application possible at all. First, the Molecular and Cellular Biology concentration's integration of research and coursework mirrors my sprint: a computational project on GitHub with regenerating figures alongside twenty hours of shadowing that taught me what a protein means to a person waiting two hours to be seen. Harvard does not let theory float free of humanity.

Second, the undergraduate research ecosystem — labs that welcome freshmen, funding that makes a summer project possible — promises what my health fair taught me to value: early, mentored responsibility. I measured 127 neighbors' blood pressure with nursing-student volunteers; the experience's value was not the numbers but the supervision that caught my first cuff placement error.

Third, the college's residential community offers a way to test my community-health education model on peers. I arrive with a curriculum that raised quiz scores 25% in my town, ready to adapt.

My grandmother still carries her bag of documents out of habit. I still carry her, through code and study, toward a future where the bag is unnecessary because the system remembers for her. Harvard is where I want to build that system.
