# Supplement - Johns Hopkins University (Why Hopkins)

**Student:** Elfin | **Major:** Molecular Biology & Medicine | **Sprint:** Day 1 Mon Aug 24 - Day 45 Wed Oct 7, 2026

## Draft (~250 words)

My first laboratory was a village clinic waiting room — two rooms, one doctor, five villages, and my grandmother's bag of documents that never matched the clinic's files. That experience taught me medicine's central problem: expertise is excellent but scarce, and scarcity is a design problem. Johns Hopkins is where I want to learn to design that scarcity away.

Three specifics pull me to Hopkins. First, the Molecular and Cellular Biology major's integration of wet lab and computational tracks mirrors how I already work: my computational project exploring protein-ligand interactions lives on GitHub with regenerating figures, but its best insight came from twenty hours shadowing at a poliklinik where I watched a doctor diagnose in minutes what paperwork consumed for an hour. Hopkins does not let code float free of patients — exactly the discipline my self-taught practice needs.

Second, the Pre-Professional Advising and extensive undergraduate research opportunities promise what my sprint health fair taught me to value: early, mentored responsibility. I organized a one-day fair for 127 neighbors with nursing-student volunteers — blood-pressure and BMI stations, education booths, a pre/post quiz showing 25% gain — and learned that permission and logistics matter as much as knowledge. At Hopkins, I want that lesson scaled: working in a lab that publishes before I graduate, as many undergraduates do.

Third, Baltimore's community health landscape offers a way to test my Turkey-trained eye elsewhere. I arrive with a paper, a repo, a shadowing log, and a fair's worth of evidence that I can extend one doctor's reach. I want to leave Hopkins able to do it for thousands.
