# Supplement - Rice University (Why Architecture)

**Student:** Azra | **Major:** Architecture | **Sprint:** Day 1 Mon Aug 24 - Day 45 Wed Oct 7, 2026

## Draft (~250 words)

My first architecture studio was my grandmother's kitchen in Balat — 9 square meters where three women negotiate a crack in the wall, a marble sill worn concave by elbows, and a doorway that survived 1999 while the building next door didn't. I have been drawing that kitchen since I was six, trying to understand why some spaces hold people and others fail them. Rice's School of Architecture is where I want to learn to answer that question at the scale it deserves.

Three specifics pull me to Rice. First, the preceptorship year — a full year working in an office between the fourth and fifth year — promises what my self-taught SketchUp hours cannot: learning how a line becomes a beam, a beam becomes a budget, a budget becomes a building that must actually stand. My 45-day portfolio sprint taught me to make quickly; preceptorship will teach me to make responsibly.

Second, Rice's studio culture treats Houston as a laboratory. The city is Balat at another latitude: layered, humid, unequal, full of bus stops that deserve better. I want to study in a program that sends students to measure such places rather than imagine them from photographs — the same impulse that sent me to observe the Kadıköy stop at peak and off-peak, counting where people actually stood when the timetable had faded.

Third, the college system's intimacy matters for a full-aid international student who builds best in small, accountable communities. My Tasarım ve Paylaş workshop worked because twenty people knew each other's strengths; Rice's colleges promise that same human scale at the level of a world-class research university.

I arrive with a sketchbook full of measured cracks and a courtyard library nook built from reclaimed pallets. I want to leave Rice able to draw buildings worthy of my grandmother's trust.
