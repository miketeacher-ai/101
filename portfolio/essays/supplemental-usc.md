# Supplement - USC School of Architecture

**Student:** Azra | **Major:** Architecture | **Sprint:** Day 1 Mon Aug 24 - Day 45 Wed Oct 7, 2026

## Draft (~250 words)

USC asks applicants to describe how they would contribute to its community — a question my last 45 days have answered in wood and chalk. I arrived at architecture through a crack in my grandmother's Balat wall, learned it by measuring the bus stop where I waited for school, and tested it by building a small library nook in our courtyard with twenty peers and reclaimed pallets. Each project was an argument that public space deserves the same care as private monuments.

That argument is why USC fits. First, Los Angeles is a city where infrastructure is the architecture — where a bus stop, like the Kadıköy one I redesigned, determines whether a neighborhood feels possible after dark. USC's emphasis on urban engagement and its location in a city rebuilding its transit imagination mirrors the questions my work raises: how does a transit shelter earn women's trust at 10 p.m., how does shade become civic infrastructure in a warming city?

Second, USC's shop culture — full-scale fabrication as a mode of thought, not just output — is the environment my self-taught modeling needs. My SketchUp models improved when I started building 1:20 cardboard mock-ups; my pavilion concept for seismic resilience only made sense when I could feel its joint in my hands. I want four years of that feedback loop at the scale only a program with USC's fabrication resources can provide.

Third, the community I built — Tasarım ve Paylaş, twenty students designing for each other — is the community I seek: diverse in income and language, united by the belief that drawing is a form of citizenship. At USC I would contribute the same habit: noticing who waits where, and drawing them a better place to stand.
