# SAT Vocabulary — Words in Context Mastery

Digital SAT never asks definitions. It tests whether you can pick the *precise* word for a context — meaning + tone + part of speech. Learn words as collocations with connotation, not as translations. Train in `apps/vocab-trainer.html` (SAT filter).

## How it's tested
1. **Words in Context:** "Which choice best maintains the tone/style?" — precision beats synonymy.
2. **Textual/Quantitative claims:** vocabulary of *research logic*: corroborate, substantiate, confound, caveat.
3. **Trap design:** two choices are near-synonyms; the *register* (formal/neutral/strong) decides.

## Core 60 — high-frequency exam words (Tier A)
**Research & argument:** corroborate, substantiate, refute, undermine, assert, contend, concede, imply, infer, ambiguous
**Change & effect:** diminish, augment, exacerbate, mitigate, precipitate, curtail, sustain, reverse, offset, yield
**Attitude & tone:** skeptical, indifferent, ambivalent, pragmatic, candid, wary, dismissive, emphatic, measured, impartial
**Complexity & nuance:** nuanced, arbitrary, inherent, paradoxical, unprecedented, superficial, disproportionate, contingent, viable, tenuous
**Behavior & society:** conform, deviate, adhere to, resist, exploit, accommodate, integrate, alienate, advocate, deter
*Standard:* use each correctly in one sentence about a real article you read this week.

## Advanced 40 — top-percentile precision (Tier B)
| Word | Precise meaning | Tone | Exam-style use |
|---|---|---|---|
| equivocal | open to multiple interpretations; unclear | neutral | "The data remained equivocal, so the team repeated the trial." |
| corroborating | confirming with independent evidence | positive | "Corroborating records settled the dispute." |
| spurious | falsely seeming genuine | negative | "The correlation proved spurious once controls were added." |
| tenuous | weak, flimsy (of claims/links) | negative | "A tenuous connection between the two events." |
| circumspect | cautious and considered | positive | "Circumspect investors avoided the bubble." |
| dogmatic | asserting opinions as facts | negative | "Dogmatic claims rarely survive peer review." |
| pragmatic | practical over principled | positive | "A pragmatic fix beat the elegant one." |
| salient | standing out as most important | neutral | "The salient finding was the age gap." |
| substantiate | support with evidence | positive | "Only one study substantiated the claim." |
| conflate | merge two distinct things (in error) | negative | "The article conflates correlation with cause." |

## Morphemes (decode unfamiliar words in 5 s)
- **bene-/mal-** good/bad → benign, beneficial / malicious, malfeasance
- **-amb-/amphi-** both → ambiguous, ambivalent, amphibious
- **contra-/counter-** against → contradict, counteract
- **-circum-** around → circumvent, circumspect
- **super-/sub-** above/below → superficial / subordinate
- **-ject-** throw → reject, conjecture, eject
- **-dict-** say → predict, contradict, verdict
- **-spec-/spic-** look → circumspect, conspicuous, retrospect
- **-ver-** truth → verify, veracity
- **-ten-/tin-** hold → tenuous, retain, tenacious

## Connotation ladders (the trap-decider)
- cautious < **circumspect** < paranoid
- strange < **unconventional** < bizarre
- unclear < **equivocal** < deceptive
- support < **substantiate** < propagandize
- brave < **audacious** < reckless
Exam logic: the passage's tone picks the rung. Neutral science passage → middle rung almost always.
